chop

maximilian becht · final project year 1

This project investigates how collectively reappropriated, low-tech systems can serve as tools for democratic resource management and community self-determination. In response to increasing social and ecological fragmentation driven by centralized and growth-dependent infrastructures, it seeks to develop and document technologies that enable small-scale autonomy, collective maintenance, and knowledge sharing.

Drawing from degrowth, the project rejects the notion that progress must be tied to increased complexity, wealth, and consumption. Instead, it employs simplicity, sustainability and local adaptability as design values. Technologies are treated not as neutral tools, but as political objects that shape relations — either reinforcing dependence on corporate systems, or enabling dynamic forms of cooperation. By treating resources such as water, tools and knowledge as commons — collectively appropriated and regulated — the project aligns with historical and contemporary practices that resist privatization. It views autonomy not as isolation, but as interdependence structured by mutual trust and shared responsibility.
